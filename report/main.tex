\begin{abstract}
We predict myocardial infarction or coronary heart disease (MICHD) from the 2015 Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System: 328{,}135 labeled respondents, 321 survey features, and a positive rate of 8.8\%.
Because accuracy is dominated by the negative class, model selection uses the F1 score of the disease class (F1$+$).
All estimators are implemented in NumPy from the CS-433 lectures.
On a held-out validation fold we compare six linear methods and retain logistic regression trained by batch gradient descent (200 steps, step size $0.1$) with decision threshold $t^\ast=0.23$ (validation F1$+$ $=0.4176$).
The same hyperparameters are then used to refit preprocessing and weights on the full training set.
The resulting submission attains public F1$+$ $=0.432$ (accuracy $0.871$) on AIcrowd.
The unlabeled test set is used only for this final prediction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

We treat MICHD screening as binary classification: $y=+1$ if a BRFSS respondent reports myocardial infarction or coronary heart disease, and $y=-1$ otherwise~\cite{brfss2015,aicrowd_p1}.
Of 328{,}135 training labels, 28{,}975 are positive.
A constant predictor of $-1$ therefore reaches accuracy $0.911$ with zero recall on the disease class, so we report F1$+$, the metric used by the course leaderboard.

The implementation comprises the six required estimators in \texttt{implementations.py}~\cite{cs433}, a leakage-aware preprocessor in \texttt{src/preprocess.py}, and a two-phase driver \texttt{run.py} controlled by \texttt{config.yaml}.
Phase~1 selects the method and the decision threshold on a validation split.
Phase~2 refits the chosen model on all labeled rows and writes the competition file.
This report follows the Project~1 IEEE layout~\cite{epfl_latex_template}.

\section{Models and Methods}

\paragraph{Data and preprocessing.}
The training matrix has 328{,}135 rows and 321 columns; the test matrix has 109{,}379 rows.
About 44.8\% of entries are missing before cleaning.
Following an exploratory analysis (\texttt{doc/eda.ipynb}), we discard 143 columns and retain 178: survey identifiers and sampling weights, duplicate anthropometric fields, modules with more than 90\% missing values, and leakage items such as \texttt{HAREHAB1} that are consequences of a cardiac diagnosis rather than predictors.
BRFSS sentinel codes (77, 99, 777, and analogues) are mapped to missing.
Codes 7 and 9 are treated as ``don't know'' or ``refused'' except on age variables, where they are valid groups; for \texttt{\_AGEG5YR} only code 14 is missing.
For \texttt{PHYSHLTH}, \texttt{MENTHLTH}, \texttt{POORHLTH}, and \texttt{CHILDREN}, code 88 (none) is mapped to zero.
Each retained column is imputed by its training median and standardized to zero mean and unit variance on the same rows.
A column of ones is then prepended, so the linear predictor is $x^\top w$ with an intercept.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.}
Labeled rows are shuffled with seed $0$ and split 80/20 into a training fold and a validation fold \emph{before} any median or scale is computed, so validation rows do not enter the preprocessing statistics.
Phase~1 trains each candidate on the 80\% fold only.
For every fitted weight vector we evaluate 51 decision thresholds on an evenly spaced grid $t\in[0.05,0.55]$ and retain the value $t^\ast$ that maximizes validation F1$+$.
For ridge regression the same search is repeated over 12 values of the penalty, $\lambda\in[10^{-4},10]$, on a logarithmic grid.
Phase~2 does not repeat this search.
It refits the preprocessor and the selected estimator on all 328{,}135 labeled rows, using the hyperparameters chosen in phase~1, and applies the fixed threshold to the test scores.
Test labels are never observed.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Let $TP$, $FP$, $TN$, and $FN$ be the confusion counts for the disease class $y=+1$, and let $N=TP+TN+FP+FN$.
Accuracy, positive-class recall, precision, and F1 are
\begin{align}
\mathrm{Acc}&=\frac{TP+TN}{N},
&
\mathrm{Rec}^{+}&=\frac{TP}{TP+FN}, \nonumber\\
\mathrm{Prec}^{+}&=\frac{TP}{TP+FP},
&
\mathrm{F1}^{+}&=\frac{2\,\mathrm{Prec}^{+}\,\mathrm{Rec}^{+}}{\mathrm{Prec}^{+}+\mathrm{Rec}^{+}}.
\label{eq:metrics}
\end{align}
Model selection uses $\mathrm{F1}^{+}$.

\paragraph{Candidate estimators.}
All six candidates are linear in the preprocessed features.
The first four use labels in $\{+1,-1\}$ and the mean squared error
\begin{equation}
L(w)=\frac{1}{2N}\|y-Xw\|_2^2,
\quad
\nabla L(w)=\frac{1}{N}X^\top(Xw-y).
\label{eq:mse}
\end{equation}
Their updates are
\begin{align}
w &\leftarrow w-\gamma\nabla L(w),
\label{eq:gd}\\
w &\leftarrow w-\gamma\, x_n(x_n^\top w-y_n),
\label{eq:sgd}\\
X^\top X\, w &= X^\top y,
\label{eq:ls}\\
(X^\top X+2N\lambda I)\,w &= X^\top y.
\label{eq:ridge}
\end{align}
\eqref{eq:gd} is batch GD with $\gamma=0.1$ for 200 steps.
\eqref{eq:sgd} is SGD: one row $n$ is drawn at each step, with $\gamma=0.01$ for 200 steps.
\eqref{eq:ls} is least squares.
\eqref{eq:ridge} is ridge regression, which adds $\lambda\|w\|_2^2$ to \eqref{eq:mse}; $\lambda$ is chosen on the validation grid.
These four models predict $+1$ when $x^\top w\ge t$.

Logistic regression maps labels to $\{0,1\}$ and uses
$\sigma(z)=(1+e^{-z})^{-1}$ and $\hat p(y=1\mid x)=\sigma(x^\top w)$.
The mean negative log-likelihood and its gradient are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L(w)&=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Big[
y_n\log\sigma(x_n^\top w)\\
&\quad+(1-y_n)\log\big(1-\sigma(x_n^\top w)\big)\Big],\\
\nabla L(w)&=\frac{1}{N}X^\top\big(\sigma(Xw)-y\big).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:nll}
\end{equation}
Weights start at zero and take 200 steps of size $\gamma=0.1$.
Regularized logistic regression uses the same iteration on
\begin{equation}
\nabla L(w)+2\lambda w,
\qquad
\lambda=2.85\times 10^{-4},
\label{eq:reglog}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda$ fixed from a prior validation sweep; phase~1 then selects only $t^\ast$.
Both logistic models predict $+1$ when $\hat p\ge t$.

\paragraph{Selected model.}
Unregularized logistic regression is the submitted system, with $t^\ast=0.23$.
Phase~2 repeats that optimization on all labeled rows and applies the same threshold to the test probabilities.
The regularized variant is reported as a comparison and is not the uploaded model.

\section{Results}

Table~\ref{tab:methods} reports phase-1 validation F1$+$ for the six candidates under the protocol above.
Squared-error methods remain weak once the threshold is chosen for F1$+$: least squares and full-batch MSE reach about $0.06$, and 200 steps of SGD reach $0.144$.
Ridge regression improves to $0.378$ at $\lambda=3.51$ and $t^\ast=0.05$.
Regularized logistic regression, at the fixed penalty $\lambda=2.85\times 10^{-4}$ and $t^\ast=0.23$, reaches $0.4179$.
Logistic regression at $t^\ast=0.23$ attains validation F1$+$ $=0.4176$ (accuracy $0.865$, recall $0.543$, precision $0.339$).
The default cutoff $0.5$ on the same probabilities yields F1$+$ $=0.192$, because most positive-class scores lie below one half.

\section{Discussion}

Validation and public test disagree only slightly ($0.4176$ versus $0.432$), which is consistent with refitting on the rows that were held out during tuning.
Accuracy on the public test is $0.871$ and remains a poor summary of the same predictions.
The gain from $t=0.5$ to $t^\ast=0.23$ shows that class imbalance is handled here by the decision threshold, not by changing the loss.
Squared-error estimators, including a tuned ridge penalty, do not match the logistic likelihood on this rare positive class.
Adding $\lambda=2.85\times 10^{-4}$ to the logistic gradient changes validation F1$+$ only in the fourth decimal ($0.4179$ versus $0.4176$).
On the public test the same penalty scores $0.431$ (accuracy $0.870$), below the unregularized submission ($0.432$, accuracy $0.871$), so it does not replace that file.
Further improvement would require representations that are not linear in the raw survey codes; products of standardized codes did not raise validation F1$+$ in our checks.

\section{Summary}

We select logistic regression by validation F1$+$ and submit a full-data refit at $t^\ast=0.23$.
To reproduce the file, place the three competition CSVs in \texttt{data/}, set \texttt{config.yaml} to \texttt{phase: 2}, \texttt{method: logistic}, and \texttt{threshold: 0.23}, run \texttt{python run.py}, and upload \texttt{outputs/submission\_baseline.csv}.
